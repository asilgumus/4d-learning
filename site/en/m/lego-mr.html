LEGO MRI

A step-by-step build guide for a rebuild of LEGO's own MRI set using SPIKE Prime parts, plus a preparation guide for explaining the MRI to a child.

Children going into an MRIBuild 2–4 hours

Build guide

1. Frame and base

A long base (at least 16×32 studs), the machine's two side columns and the top beam. The MRI machine should look like a "ring": the tunnel wide enough for a minifigure to fit (at least 6 studs).

2. Tunnel ring

Build the round ring with horizontal half circles and plates. Leave the inside open. Put a flat panel with the machine's name in front of the ring.

3. Sliding bed

Build a rail on the base that passes through the tunnel. The patient bed slides on the rail; it can be pushed by a SPIKE Prime motor or by hand.

4. SPIKE Prime movement

Build a mechanism with a large motor and gears that slowly moves the bed into and out of the tunnel. Hide the hub in the control room. One button press moves the bed in, another moves it out.

5. Control room

Add a small room with a technician minifigure and a screen (glass or sticker). Let the child see from the model that "the technician can see me".

6. Sound and light (optional)

You can program a soft "tak tak" sound with the hub speaker and a blinking "scan in progress" light with the light matrix. Keep the volume as low as the child is comfortable with.

Check when done

  • Can the minifigure lie comfortably on the bed?
  • Does the bed move in and out of the tunnel without catching?
  • Is the motor too fast? (so the child is not scared)
  • Are all edges and small parts safe for the child?

How do we explain the MRI to a child?

What is an MRI?

An MRI is a machine that uses a strong magnet and radio waves to take pictures of the inside of the body. It does not hurt and gives no radiation. The child just lies still.

What will you hear?

The machine makes loud "tak tak" and "vzzz" sounds. Headphones or earplugs are provided. The sounds show that the machine is working.

What do you need to do?

Lie completely still. During the scan our toy figure stays still too; play the "statue game".

Are you alone?

No. The technician can see and hear you. Often an adult can wait beside you.

Note for families

  • This content does not replace the hospital's guidelines. Follow your hospital's instructions on sedation, fasting and metal objects.
  • Build the model with your child a few days before the scan and play it out, swapping roles (the child is the patient, you are the technician).
  • If the hospital allows, ask to see the MRI room beforehand.
