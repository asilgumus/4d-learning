Technology Addiction

A 14-slide presentation on the effects on attention, sleep, relationships and learning, with a family 'digital balance agreement' activity.

Primary schoolMiddle school45 min

Presentation

Is the screen running us, or are we running the screen?

Technology addiction workshop · 4th Dimension 6429

Technology isn't bad.

The problem is using it without noticing when and why.

Today's plan

  • We'll look at our own habits
  • We'll learn why our brain gets hooked on screens
  • We'll make small decisions for healthy limits
  • We'll prepare an agreement with our family

Warm-up: stand up

How many minutes after waking up did you first look at your phone today? Show with your hand: 0–5 min, 5–30 min, more than 30 min. Nobody is judged, we just notice.

On purpose or on autopilot?

  • On purpose: I have a goal and I stop when I'm done
  • Automatic: my hand goes to the phone by itself
  • The urge to 'check' when a notification sounds is automatic
  • Noticing is the first step to changing

How do apps keep you?

Design tricks

  • Infinite scroll: the page never ends
  • Autoplay: the next video starts by itself
  • Notifications: they call your attention back
  • Like counts: they feed the need for approval

What you can do

  • Set a time limit before you start scrolling
  • Turn off autoplay
  • Silence unnecessary notifications
  • Measure how much you enjoyed it, not the likes

What happens in our brain?

  • New and surprising things create a pleasure signal in the brain
  • Screens deliver this within seconds, again and again
  • A brain used to constant fast stimulation can get bored with slow tasks (books, lessons)
  • Good news: habits can be reshaped

Sleep and screens

  • Screen light before bed can delay falling asleep
  • A sleepy brain struggles to pay attention and manage emotions
  • Rule: screens off at least 1 hour before bed
  • Charge the phone in the living room, not the bedroom

Attention, lessons and relationships

  • A phone on the table splits our attention even when silent
  • Doing two things at once slows down both
  • A screen during meals and conversation can send the message 'you aren't important'
  • Screen-free time together strengthens bonds

Warning signs

Restlessness when you don't use it, hiding how long you use it, cutting back on sleep and responsibilities, losing interest in things you love. If you notice a few of these, talk to a trusted adult.

5 small decisions for balance

  • Set a screen time limit
  • Keep meals and homework screen-free
  • Turn screens off 1 hour before bed
  • Plan one screen-free activity a week (sport, games, nature)
  • When bored, check your 'to-do list' instead of your phone

Digital balance agreement

Sit down together with your family. Fill in the printable agreement: screen-free hours, screen-free places, a rule everyone follows. Parents sign too.

Remember

The goal isn't to run away from screens. It's to use screens for our goals.
